Key business insightsClick chart elements to focus; hover for detail

Patients by medical condition

Arthritis and Diabetes lead with ~9.2K patients each; Asthma is lowest at 9,095.
Implication: The gap between highest and lowest condition is only 123 patients (1.3%), so capacity and staffing should be planned evenly across all six.

Patients by insurance provider

Cigna covers the most patients (20.3%), closely followed by Medicare and UnitedHealthcare.
Implication: No provider exceeds ~20% of patients, which limits dependence on any single payer.

Patient age profile

Average patient is 51.5 years old; the middle 50% fall between 35 and 68.
Implication: Services should serve a broad adult and older-adult population, from teenagers (13) to patients up to 89.

Billing: senior patients vs. all patients

Patients aged 60+ are billed on average the same as everyone else ($25,564 vs. $25,595).
Implication: Age is not a meaningful cost driver; cost variation must come from other factors.

Average monthly billing over time

Average billing per admission has held in a narrow band around the overall average of $25,595.
Implication: Billing per patient has stayed predictable from the start of the period (2019) to the most recent months (2024), supporting reliable revenue forecasting. Chart shows the first and latest five months reported; axis starts at $24K.

Management takeawaysWhat is happening, why it matters, what to watch

1 · Demand is broad, not concentrated

What
Each of the six conditions makes up 16.5–16.8% of admissions.
Why
No single specialty drives volume, so there is no obvious high-priority service line by patient count.
Watch
Keep staffing and capacity balanced across Arthritis, Diabetes, Hypertension, Obesity, Cancer and Asthma.

2 · Payer exposure is diversified

What
Five insurers each cover 19.7–20.3% of patients (Cigna 20.3%, Aetna 19.7%).
Why
Concentration risk is low; revenue is not tied to one payer.
Watch
A policy or contract change at any one provider would still affect roughly 1 in 5 patients.

3 · Billing is stable and not age-driven

What
Average billing is $25,595; patients 60+ average $25,564; monthly averages stayed within $24.9K–$26.3K in reported months.
Why
Cost per patient is predictable, which helps budgeting and forecasting.
Watch
Cost differences are not explained by age; other factors in the data (for example admission type, medication or length of stay) are worth examining next.

4 · Emergency capacity view still missing

What
Monthly emergency admission counts have not yet been produced.
Why
Without them, emergency staffing and bed planning rely on assumptions.
Watch
Complete this analysis before the next capacity planning cycle.
